# Assumptions & limitations (read before using any number)

**Status: illustrative prototype. The 2025 operating year is calibrated against outturn (below); the
2026-2050 pathway is not validated. Do not quote numbers as forecasts.**

* Costs/technologies in `ukabm/config.py` are indicative (order of DESNZ Generation Costs 2023); real 2024 £.
* Initial fleet c.2025 (MW) is a stylised aggregate; vintages are randomised per zone; nuclear units and dates indicative (AGR closures, Hinkley C 2030/31, Sizewell C 2038/39).
* Legacy fleet performance (calibrated to 2025): AGR availability 0.62 and Sizewell B 0.88 (GB nuclear 34.2 TWh in 2025, NESO; EDF fleet update Jan 2026); existing CCGTs 0.54 → 0.46 efficiency (GCV) falling with age, fleet mean ~0.50 (DUKES Table 5.10), new CCGTs 0.55; biomass availability 0.70 (19.9 TWh in 2025 = 60 % load factor on 3.8 GW).
* One weather/demand year (ESPENI 2024, hourly). Wind/solar capacity factors are derived by normalising ESPENI output by a high quantile (no installed-capacity series) — onshore = 0.75×, offshore = 1.10× the GB shape. No spatial weather variation.
* Temperature for heat demand is synthetic. Heat pump SPF fixed at 2.9. 0.6 m HPs and 1.2 m EVs assumed embedded in the 2024 base demand.
* Market: single GB price, perfect within-day foresight for storage/flex, no unit commitment, no ramping, no reserves; ORDC-type scarcity adder (σ = 2.5 GW) stands in for scarcity pricing. The adder's margin is physical supply minus domestic demand (exports are treated as interruptible), and an export bid below the adder is curtailed, so exports are never served above their bid.
* Interconnectors: capacity from the PyPSA-GB register with corrected commissioning years (NeuConnect 2028, GridLink 2030, FAB Link 2031, NorthConnect not built, Greenlink 2025 → 10.5 GW in 2025). One import offer and one export bid per neighbour market. Foreign price = SRMC of an existing GB CCGT × level (continent 0.68, SEM 1.10; calibrated to 2025 day-ahead price ratios) × the observed 2024 hourly price shape of each neighbour (Energy-Charts/SMARD, same weather year as the GB profile; SEM has a GB-wind-shaped proxy). Spread ±£12/MWh around the foreign price, calibrated to 2025 gross flows (imports 44 TWh, exports 15 TWh). Price calibration uses time-weighted means; the model's demand-weighted price is not like-for-like with MID's volume-weighted price. Foreign prices fall 25 % relative to the GB CCGT reference by 2050. Interconnector capacity is flat at 14.7 GW from 2031 (FAB Link) to 2050: projects without a firm date (e.g. LionLink, Nautilus, MaresConnect, Tarchon) are not in the register, while FES net-zero pathways carry substantially more capacity in the 2030s–40s, so long-run imports and exports are capped low (scenario switch `ic_growth` scales capacity but no scenario sets it).
* Exports are GB demand for the market clearing but not GB consumption: `demand_twh`, the demand-weighted price (`price_twa`) and the policy levy (CfD + RO + CM costs per MWh) use GB final consumption (base + heat pumps + EVs + electrolysers), because these costs are recovered from GB supplier volumes only.
* Carbon intensity is reported two ways: `intensity_g_kwh` (direct combustion emissions in GB / total supply) and `intensity_neso_g_kwh` (NESO accounting: CCGT 394, OCGT 651, biomass 120, imports 65 g/kWh), which is the one to compare with NESO's published CI. The import factor (the 2024–25 average implied by NESO's data) is held constant to 2050 while neighbouring grids decarbonise, so NESO-basis CI after 2025 is an upper bound (baseline 2050: ~29 TWh of imports × 65 g/kWh ≈ 4 g/kWh).
* CfD: AR-style pots (60/20/20 offshore/onshore/solar), pay-as-clear by technology, 20-year contracts, no £ budget. CM: T-4 only, linear demand curve ±1.5 GW, price cap £75/kW, CfD/RO plant excluded but netted off the target. Battery ancillary revenue £20k/MW/yr declining.
* Network stage (every 5 years in scenario runs, every year in `compare_outturn.py`): BM-style redispatch of the wholesale schedule on the PyPSA-GB 20-zone network, solved as a transport model (no KVL) with HiGHS. Each plant can be turned down at its *bid* and turned up at its *offer*. Bids: thermal = SRMC (fuel saved); RO renewables = −ROC value; CfD renewables = −(strike − market price), the difference payment lost (positive above the strike, where turning down saves the payback); merchant renewables/hydro = 0; nuclear = −£300/MWh (never turned down in practice). Offers = the plant's wholesale offer, raised where needed to at least its bid (BSC: offer ≥ bid), so the LP cannot turn a unit down and up for profit. No BM premia (offer multipliers/adders above SRMC), so the model is compared with outturn excluding the ~£0.54bn of premia. Storage stays at its market position (spread by demand share). Interconnector imports and exports are fixed injections at their GB landing zones (register `zone`, each market's flow split by capacity: IFA, ElecLink, BritNed and Nemo in Z15, IFA2 Z16, NSL Z7, Viking Z12, EastWest Z9, Greenlink Z13, Moyle Z5); interconnector counter-trading is not modelled. Load the redispatch cannot serve behind a constraint is reported as `network_shed_twh` and is not costed (in practice it would be resolved by interconnector counter-trades or storage, which this stage holds fixed).
  * Scottish boundary capabilities B0, B2, B4, B5, B6 (MW including HVDC) for 2025/2030/2035 from NESO's Holistic Transition build-out as tabulated by LCP Delta, *From Bottlenecks to Balance* (Mar 2026, p.58), interpolated and held after 2035. The PyPSA-GB zonal links carry future HND-era values (e.g. 22 GW across Scotland–England), so these are replaced. HVDC links are steps at target energisation (Caithness–Moray, Shetland, Western Isles 2030, Western HVDC, EGL1 2027, EGL2 2029, EGL3/4 2031; SSEN Transmission, Ofgem ASTI). English and Welsh links keep their PyPSA-GB capacities.
  * HVDC landings and plant near a boundary are placed by which side of the boundary they are on, not by nearest bus. Hunterston and Torness are south of B5 and north of B6 (SP Energy Networks, RIIO-T2 Business Plan Annex 4, 2019). So the Western HVDC and EGL1 start in Z6 and cross B6 only, and the Torness AGR sits in Z6. Heysham 2 is in Lancashire (Z9). The Western Isles link lands at Beauly, south of B0, on the radial Z1_4–Z1_2 link. The PyPSA-GB Z1_3–Z1_2 corridor is closed because it would bypass B0. `BOUNDARY_NORTH` in `ukabm/data/pypsagb.py` defines each boundary cut, and a test checks that only the listed links cross it.
  * Monthly available share of boundary capability (planned outages) from LCP Delta (2026, p.59; NESO day-ahead constraint limits 2019–24), with an extra 0.76 derate on Scottish boundaries in 2025 for upgrade outages (LCP Delta p.18; NESO 2025 Annual Balancing Costs Report). B5 has its own, lower profile. It shares the Z7–Z2 HVDC corridor with B2/B4/B6, which follow the Scottish profile, so B5's AC link is derated further until the whole B5 cut is at the B5 share.
  * Hours: 96 per network year, stratified by GB wind CF into 8 equal-probability strata (12 hours each, weight = stratum size / 12), seeded by year; costs and volumes are annualised with these weights.
  * Redispatch is fed back into reporting only: `bm_net_<tech>` (TWh, + = turned up), `curtailment_twh` and `intensity_neso_post_bm_g_kwh`. Settlement, investment and the headline wholesale CI are not changed by it.
  * Siting: GB keeps national wholesale pricing (REMA decision, July 2025), so new plant is sited by resource share (PyPSA-GB DUKES split) only and does not see the network stage's zonal prices. Scenario switch `locational_signal=True` is a zonal-pricing sensitivity: resource share × zonal price / mean (weighted median over the sample hours), clipped to [0.2, 2]. With BM-based zonal prices this is a strong signal (Scottish zones price at ~£0 in 2025), so it moves most new Scottish wind south. TNUoS locational charges are not modelled.
  * Limitations: one GB wind shape for every zone (no Scottish vs English weather), so the model curtails by boundary capability rather than by local weather; no voltage, stability or inertia constraints (only thermal boundary limits); constraint cost in 2030+ depends strongly on the assumed network build-out dates.
* Gas network: allowed revenue £5.5bn/yr, –1 %/yr after 2030; non-domestic demand –1.5 %/yr.
* Retail: electricity = HP-weighted wholesale + £70 network + levies + £25 supplier; gas = wholesale + network tariff + £12.
* Single seed in the published results; ABM outcomes are path-dependent — run several seeds.

## 2025 calibration vs outturn
`python scripts/compare_outturn.py` writes [results/calibration_2025.md](../results/calibration_2025.md) (outturn
built by `scripts/fetch_outturn.py` from NESO's historic generation mix and Elexon MID / FUELHH). Known,
explained residuals in the 2025 comparison:
* **Wind +8–9 TWh / gas −8 TWh**: the wholesale stage is a copperplate, so it does not curtail Scottish wind.
  In 2025 ~8.8 TWh was curtailed in northern Scotland alone (Montel 2025 curtailment report) and replaced by
  gas in England through the Balancing Mechanism; NESO's mix is after those actions. The network stage
  now reports the redispatch. After BM redispatch, 2025 wind is 87.9 TWh (outturn 85.8), gas is 74.5 TWh
  (77.3) and NESO-basis CI is 121 g/kWh (130). Constraint cost is £0.85bn against £1.83bn outturn. The
  outturn includes ~£0.54bn of BM premia that the model does not represent; without them it is £1.29bn.
  Renewable turn-down is 6.5 TWh vs 10.4 TWh (LCP Delta 2026, from NESO data;
  `data/outturn/gb_constraints.csv`). Both are low, plausibly because every zone shares one GB wind
  shape, generation is sited at zone level, and interconnector counter-trades are not modelled.
* **CI (NESO basis) ~17 g/kWh low before redispatch, ~9 g/kWh low after**: the curtailment effect above (+9 g/kWh) plus 4.7 TWh of "other"
  generation (CHP, waste, oil; NESO factor 300 g/kWh, ~+5 g/kWh) that the model does not represent.
* **Price ~£5/MWh high, distribution too narrow**: no low-price/negative hours (no unit commitment,
  must-run or inflexibility; GB had 244 negative hours in 2025) and no scarcity tail (p95 £100 vs £123).
  Gas in 2025 is £32/MWh_th in the model vs ~£31 NBP (~90p/therm).
* **Hydro +2 TWh, storage +2 TWh**: NESO's mix counts transmission-connected hydro and storage only.
