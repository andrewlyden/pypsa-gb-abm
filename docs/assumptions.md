# Assumptions & limitations (read before using any number)

**Status: research prototype. The operating model is calibrated on 2025 and checked on 2021–2024; the
first auctions and the 2030–2050 fleet are compared with the auctions held and with published pathways
([how parameters are set and checked](calibration.md)). Numbers are not forecasts.** Every constant is
listed with its status in the [parameter register](parameters.md), every dataset with its licence in
the [data register](data.md).

* Costs of wind, solar and gas plant are DESNZ Electricity Generation Costs 2025 (2024 prices; `data/parameters/cost_paths.csv`): capital cost with financing during construction, fixed cost with insurance and connection and use-of-system charges. Nuclear, biomass, hydro and storage costs are indicative; battery capital cost (£600/kW for 2 h) has no source yet.
* Required returns are CEPA's (2025) hurdle rates by technology for contracted revenue (7.6 % onshore wind and solar, 8.9 % offshore wind, gas and batteries), 1.5 points more for merchant revenue, shifted by a firm's own cost of capital. CEPA gives ±1.1–1.7 points of uncertainty.
* Starting fleet: GB capacity in 2025 from DUKES 2026 (mean of end 2024 and end 2025); vintages, locations and owners from the DUKES station list; support from the CfD register and the RO closure rules; 32 GW of wind and solar with a CfD but not yet built (`data/fleet/`). The match of contracts to plant is by round and year, not by project. Existing gas plant closes on age at 40 (CCGT) or 45 years (assumed) or after two loss-making years without a capacity agreement. AGRs close in March 2030 (EDF, Jan 2026; Centrica, July 2026); Hinkley Point C 2030/31; Sizewell C 2038/39.
* Firms: twelve, with the plants their companies own (seven named companies, five groups; `data/fleet/firms.csv`). What a firm is like is set by one of five types (cost-of-capital spread, expectation rule, mark-up, annual budget, technologies it invests in): these are the authors' assumptions. Who holds the contracts in the pipeline is not known from the sources used; projects are given to the firms that invest in the technology in proportion to what they own.
* Existing-fleet performance (calibrated on 2025): AGR availability 0.62 and Sizewell B 0.88; existing CCGTs 0.54 → 0.46 efficiency (GCV) falling with age over 40 years, fleet mean ~0.50 (DUKES Table 5.10); biomass 3.0 GW at 0.78; hydro 1.5 GW at 0.26; "other" plant (waste, CHP) 0.7 GW at 0.65, must-run.
* Weather: one year in forward runs (2024). Hourly demand, onshore wind, offshore wind and solar are built from NESO and Elexon open data for 2021–2025 (`data/profiles/`); wind is available output (metered plus Balancing Mechanism turn-down), per MW of installed capacity. New wind and solar have a higher capacity factor than the fleet (`config.NEW_BUILD_CF`: measured for wind farms built since 2020, then DESNZ's assumption for 2030–2040), applied by raising the fleet's hourly shape to a power. No spatial weather variation in the market; regional wind profiles are in the files but not yet used.
* Exogenous drivers follow a FES 2025 pathway (`fes_pathway`, default Holistic Transition): demand other than EVs, heat pumps and electrolysis (+56 % by 2050), the EV fleet and its demand, electrolysers, interconnectors, nuclear and pumped hydro; the government's wind and solar targets are the pathway's capacities, and it tenders gas CCS and hydrogen power to the pathway's capacity (`dpa_round`, standing for Dispatchable Power Agreements and hydrogen to power). Heat pumps are the households' choice. `fes_pathway: "none"` restores the earlier assumptions.
* Temperature for heat demand is synthetic (not the weather year's). Heat pump SPF fixed at 2.9. 0.6 m HPs assumed embedded in the 2024 base demand; EV charging in the base is the pathway's 2025 value.
* Market: single GB price, perfect within-day foresight for storage/flex (storage fleet dispatched in tranches, see below), no unit commitment, no ramping, no reserves (the planner and evaluator hold them, see *Optimal vs emergent*). Offers of supported plant follow the terms of the support, as in PyPSA-GB: RO plant at minus banding × ROC price, CfDs of rounds 1–3 at minus the strike, CfDs from round 4 at zero and unpaid when the price is negative, merchant renewables at £0.5/MWh, nuclear and "other" plant at −£50/MWh. Scarcity: a stepped mark-up on the de-rated margin, the curve PyPSA-GB fitted to 2024 day-ahead prices (`config.SCARCITY_STEPS`), added to the merit price; an export bid below the mark-up is curtailed. Prices can be negative; in 2021–2025 the model has none (outturn: 244 hours in 2025), because in the cheapest hours it imports at the neighbours' price plus the interconnector spread.
* Interconnectors: capacity from the PyPSA-GB register with corrected commissioning years (NeuConnect 2028, GridLink 2030, FAB Link 2031, NorthConnect not built, Greenlink 2025 → 10.5 GW in 2025). One import offer and one export bid per neighbour market. In a historical year run on its own weather the continental price is the observed one (Energy-Charts/SMARD, ECB exchange rate). In future years: foreign price = SRMC of an existing GB CCGT × level (continent 0.68, SEM 1.10; calibrated to 2025 day-ahead price ratios) × the observed hourly price shape of each neighbour in the weather year (SEM has a GB-wind-shaped proxy). Spread ±£12/MWh around the foreign price, calibrated to 2025 gross flows (imports 44 TWh, exports 15 TWh). Price calibration uses time-weighted means; the model's demand-weighted price is not like-for-like with MID's volume-weighted price. Foreign prices fall 25 % relative to the GB CCGT reference by 2050. After 2030 capacity follows the FES pathway where it is above the register (19.4 GW in 2035 and 21.8 GW in 2050 in Holistic Transition), scaled across the existing markets; without a pathway it is flat at 14.7 GW from 2031.
* Exports are GB demand for the market clearing but not GB consumption: `demand_twh`, the demand-weighted price (`price_twa`) and the policy levy (CfD + RO + CM costs per MWh) use GB final consumption (base + heat pumps + EVs + electrolysers), because these costs are recovered from GB supplier volumes only.
* Carbon intensity is reported two ways: `intensity_g_kwh` (direct combustion emissions in GB / total supply) and `intensity_neso_g_kwh` (NESO accounting: CCGT 394, OCGT 651, biomass 120, imports 65 g/kWh), which is the one to compare with NESO's published CI. The import factor (the 2024–25 average implied by NESO's data) is held constant to 2050 while neighbouring grids decarbonise, so NESO-basis CI after 2025 is an upper bound (baseline 2050: ~29 TWh of imports × 65 g/kWh ≈ 4 g/kWh).
* CfD: AR-style pots, pay-as-clear by technology, 20-year contracts. The round is set by the government agent (`ukabm.institutions`): by default (`government: "targets"`) it tracks a capacity target path (the FES pathway's wind and solar capacity) with a £ budget, adaptive caps and a levy guard (£30/MWh), within the build-rate limit of each technology; `government: "fixed"` is the earlier rule of 6 GW a year at 60/20/20 with no £ budget. Firms bid by default on their own expectations over the life of the asset (`decision_logic: "expectations"`), and only for the technologies their type invests in. Budget impact = (strike − forecast capture price) × expected generation, floored at zero. CM: the target is set by the system operator from the 3 h reliability standard (`system_operator: "standard"`, with a T-1 auction), linear demand curve ±1.5 GW, price cap £75/kW, CfD/RO plant excluded but netted off the target; de-rating factors are NESO's for the 2030-31 T-4 auction. New build in an auction is limited by the technology's build rate. At DESNZ's 2025 costs new gas plant needs more than the price cap, so new capacity in the auction is mostly batteries. The auction stops at the first offer above the demand curve and at the end of the curve (target + 1.5 GW), so zero-priced offers are not accepted beyond it. New-build CM bids are priced on the investment forecast for t+3 (known fuel and carbon paths), not on the latest outturn year. Before November 2026 both were missing: after the 2026 gas-price spike the model procured ~98 GW against a ~50 GW target at £0/kW, almost all new CCGT. New-build batteries offer as a stepwise supply curve: each chunk's expected services revenue counts the batteries ranked ahead of it in the same auction.
* Battery revenue stack (`config.BATTERY_*`, `GBMarketModel.battery_services_per_mw`). On top of the modelled hourly day-ahead arbitrage and the CM, batteries share two pots across the battery fleet, so revenue per MW falls as the fleet grows:
  * ancillary services (frequency response and reserve), a fixed-size £100m/yr pot, i.e. £20k/MW on the 5 GW 2025 fleet. Modo Energy monthly ME BESS GB reports put frequency response at roughly £20–25k/MW/yr through 2025;
  * intraday and BM trading that the hourly day-ahead clearing does not capture, £3,960/yr per MW of wind and solar capacity (balancing and forecast-error volume scale with VRE).

  Calibration: the 2025 trading revenue (modelled arbitrage ~£5.6k/MW plus the pot) is ~£44k/MW. That is 60 % of Modo's 2-hour GB BESS index of £73k/MW/yr for the 12 months to April 2026. Per-MW revenue is not extrapolated above its 2025 level when the fleet is smaller than 5 GW. The same function is used in settlement (realised) and in investment and CM bids (expected at the fleet in the first operating year, or the delivery year for CM new build, including the pipeline and the candidate project). New-build battery CM offers form a stepwise supply curve: chunks are ranked cheapest first and each chunk's expected services count all battery MW ranked ahead of it in the same auction plus itself, so the marginal accepted chunk expects exactly the per-MW services the delivery-year fleet will share (without this, up to 5 GenCos × 4 chunks each expected the revenue of a lone entrant and one auction accepted 8 GW). The trading pot is a judgement call: with ×0.5 / ×1.5 on `BATTERY_TRADING_PER_MW_VRE` the baseline 2050 fleet is 15 / 33 GW (24 GW central), while OCGT and prices barely move.

  Limitations: other flexible providers (CCGT, DSR, interconnectors) do not compete for the pots, and the pots are not charged to consumers (no BSUoS in the retail tariff); their annual cost is reported as `battery_services_bn`. Battery capex is still £600/kW for 2 h (`config.TECHS`), which looks high against 2025 project costs. Storage is dispatched in tranches (3 for batteries), each a price-taker on the price left by earlier tranches, and a tranche skips days on which its own trades would lose money. This stops the fleet trading away its own spread, which had given negative realised arbitrage for fleets above ~8 GW.
* Network stage (every 5 years in scenario runs, every year in `compare_outturn.py`): BM-style redispatch of the wholesale schedule on the PyPSA-GB 20-zone network, solved as a transport model (no KVL) with HiGHS. Each plant can be turned down at its *bid* and turned up at its *offer*. Bids: thermal = SRMC (fuel saved); RO renewables = −ROC value; CfD renewables = −(strike − market price), the difference payment lost (positive above the strike, where turning down saves the payback); merchant renewables/hydro = 0; nuclear = −£300/MWh (never turned down in practice). Offers = the plant's wholesale offer, raised where needed to at least its bid (BSC: offer ≥ bid), so the LP cannot turn a unit down and up for profit. No BM premia (offer multipliers/adders above SRMC), so the model is compared with outturn excluding the ~£0.54bn of premia. Storage stays at its market position (spread by demand share). Interconnector imports and exports are fixed injections at their GB landing zones (register `zone`, each market's flow split by capacity: IFA, ElecLink, BritNed and Nemo in Z15, IFA2 Z16, NSL Z7, Viking Z12, EastWest Z9, Greenlink Z13, Moyle Z5); interconnector counter-trading is not modelled. Load the redispatch cannot serve behind a constraint is reported as `network_shed_twh` and is not costed (in practice it would be resolved by interconnector counter-trades or storage, which this stage holds fixed).
  * Scottish boundary capabilities B0, B2, B4, B5, B6 (MW including HVDC) for 2025/2030/2035 from NESO's Holistic Transition build-out as tabulated by LCP Delta, *From Bottlenecks to Balance* (Mar 2026, p.58), interpolated and held after 2035. The PyPSA-GB zonal links carry future HND-era values (e.g. 22 GW across Scotland–England), so these are replaced. HVDC links are steps at target energisation (Caithness–Moray, Shetland, Western Isles 2030, Western HVDC, EGL1 2027, EGL2 2029, EGL3/4 2031; SSEN Transmission, Ofgem ASTI). English and Welsh links keep their PyPSA-GB capacities.
  * HVDC landings and plant near a boundary are placed by which side of the boundary they are on, not by nearest bus. Hunterston and Torness are south of B5 and north of B6 (SP Energy Networks, RIIO-T2 Business Plan Annex 4, 2019). So the Western HVDC and EGL1 start in Z6 and cross B6 only, and the Torness AGR sits in Z6. Heysham 2 is in Lancashire (Z9). The Western Isles link lands at Beauly, south of B0, on the radial Z1_4–Z1_2 link. The PyPSA-GB Z1_3–Z1_2 corridor is closed because it would bypass B0. `BOUNDARY_NORTH` in `ukabm/data/pypsagb.py` defines each boundary cut, and a test checks that only the listed links cross it.
  * Monthly available share of boundary capability (planned outages) from LCP Delta (2026, p.59; NESO day-ahead constraint limits 2019–24), with an extra 0.76 derate on Scottish boundaries in 2025 for upgrade outages (LCP Delta p.18; NESO 2025 Annual Balancing Costs Report). B5 has its own, lower profile. It shares the Z7–Z2 HVDC corridor with B2/B4/B6, which follow the Scottish profile, so B5's AC link is derated further until the whole B5 cut is at the B5 share.
  * Hours: 96 per network year, stratified by GB wind CF into 8 equal-probability strata (12 hours each, weight = stratum size / 12), seeded by year; costs and volumes are annualised with these weights.
  * Redispatch is fed back into reporting only: `bm_net_<tech>` (TWh, + = turned up), `curtailment_twh` and `intensity_neso_post_bm_g_kwh`. Settlement, investment and the headline wholesale CI are not changed by it.
  * Siting: GB keeps national wholesale pricing (REMA decision, July 2025), so new plant is sited by resource share (PyPSA-GB DUKES split) only and does not see the network stage's zonal prices. Scenario switch `locational_signal=True` is a zonal-pricing sensitivity: resource share × zonal price / mean (weighted median over the sample hours), clipped to [0.2, 2]. With BM-based zonal prices this is a strong signal (Scottish zones price at ~£0 in 2025), so it moves most new Scottish wind south. TNUoS locational charges are not modelled.
  * Limitations: one GB wind shape for every zone (no Scottish vs English weather), so the model curtails by boundary capability rather than by local weather; no voltage, stability or inertia constraints (only thermal boundary limits); constraint cost in 2030+ depends strongly on the assumed network build-out dates.
* Gas price (central, `config.GAS_PATH_CENTRAL`, GBP/MWh_th): 2025 29.4 (outturn £30.45 in 2024 prices) · 2026 55 · 2027 63 · 2028 45 · 2030 35 · 2035 29.2 · 2050 25, linear between points. Re-based in November 2026 for the 2026 spike: NBP spot 218–234 p/therm in November 2026 (Trading Economics), Q1-27 forward ~223 p/therm and Summer-27 ~160 p/therm on 25 Nov 2026 (Catalyst Commercial). The 2026 annual mean and the unwinding after 2027 are assumptions (no reliable 2028 quote); from 2035 the path is the pre-spike one (32 → 25). `high_gas` adds £15/MWh_th to every year. Agents know the fuel path (forecasts use it); only carbon expectations differ between agents.
* Gas network: allowed revenue £5.5bn/yr, –1 %/yr after 2030; non-domestic demand –1.5 %/yr.
* Retail: electricity = HP-weighted wholesale + £70 network + levies + £25 supplier; gas = wholesale + network tariff + £12.
* Ensembles (`ukabm.ensemble`): seeds vary agent/path draws (vintages, CfD/RO split, bid noise, siting, merchant order, households), not weather, demand, fuel or carbon paths. Bands are min–max across seeds, not confidence intervals.

## Checks against outturn, auctions and published pathways
[docs/calibration.md](calibration.md) says what is calibrated (a handful of operating parameters, on
2025) and reports the three checks: the operating back-cast for 2021–2025
([results/backcast.md](../results/backcast.md)), the first auctions against Allocation Round 7 and the
T-4 auction for 2029-30 ([results/auction_check.md](../results/auction_check.md)), and the baseline's
2030–2050 fleet and demand against FES 2025, the CCC and Clean Power 2030
([results/pathways_comparison.md](../results/pathways_comparison.md)). Known residuals: the price
distribution is too narrow (no negative-price hours); prices are 12 % low in the gas-crisis years; the
model's offshore wind strike is about 20 % above Round 7 and its onshore strike 13 % below; households
adopt far fewer heat pumps than the pathways assume.

## Optimal vs emergent (`ukabm.costs`, `ukabm.planner`, `ukabm.gap`)
* **System cost** is a GB resource cost, the same definition for every run: annuitised capex of plant online after 2025 at the social discount rate (3.5 %, HM Treasury Green Book), fixed O&M of all active plant, fuel and VOM, CO2 × carbon price, imports at their offer price minus exports at their bid price, unserved energy × VoLL (£6,000/MWh), minus electrolyser consumption × its willingness to pay. CfD, RO and CM payments, scarcity rents and mark-ups are transfers and cancel. Pre-2025 capital is sunk. Constraint (redispatch) cost is not included yet: both runs are costed copperplate.
* **Planner (Run 1)**: PyPSA multi-investment-period LP, single GB bus, investment periods 2025, 2030, …, 2050 (each standing for 5 years), perfect foresight, 3.5 % discount rate. Same technologies, costs, fuel/carbon paths, 2024 weather profiles, interconnector markets and electrolyser capacity as the agents; starts from the agent run's initial fleet (incl. Hinkley C and Sizewell C) and meets that run's realised inflexible demand (base + heat pumps + EVs) in each period, so demand-side choices are held equal. Hours: a stratified sample of days, one in seven plus for every period the eight days with the highest peak and the eight with the highest mean of load net of the pathway's wind and solar, each extreme day standing for itself (about 1,560 h per period, weighted to 8,760). With one peak day per period, as before November 2026, the plan was short of firm capacity on the full year: a plan made at a higher value of lost load was £0.3bn cheaper on the default yardstick; with the extreme days the default plan is the cheaper by £0.1bn, and solves faster. The planner starts with the contracted pipeline and the pathway's nuclear and pumped hydro, like the agents, but not with the gas CCS and hydrogen plant the agents' government tenders. No new build in the first period or before a technology's lead time; build-rate limits per year (`config.PLANNER_BUILD_MW_PER_YEAR`: offshore 6 GW, onshore 2.5, solar 6, battery 5, CCGT 3, OCGT 2, gas CCS 1.5, H2 turbine 1.5; no new nuclear beyond the committed units, which agents cannot build either). Since November 2026 the firms face the same limits per year (`agent_build_limits`). The planner keeps the existing fleet to its end of life (no early retirement), and builds continuous (not lumpy) capacity.
* **Evaluator**: both fleets are dispatched at least cost over all 8,760 hours of each period year (PyPSA, perfect foresight, cyclic storage) and costed with the same definition. Investment gap = evaluator(agents' fleet) − evaluator(planner's fleet); operation gap = agents' realised cost − evaluator(agents' fleet). Present values 2025–2054 at 3.5 %, each period year standing for five years.
* **Reserve and response** (both the planner and the evaluator; `config.RESERVE`, `planner._add_reserve`): every hour holds (a) frequency response of 1,800 MW, the SQSS infrequent infeed loss, on storage headroom (battery, pumped hydro) with at least an hour of stored energy behind it, and (b) operating reserve of 10 % of available wind and solar output, on headroom of flexible thermal plant (CCGT, OCGT, gas CCS, H2 turbine, biomass, hydro) and storage. Nuclear, wind and solar hold none. Headroom is capacity minus output, so an hour's reserve competes with that hour's energy; in the planner the capacity term includes new build, so the requirement can drive investment. A shortfall is allowed at £1,000/MWh (counted with unserved energy) rather than made infeasible. These sizes are simple stand-ins for NESO's dynamic response and reserve products, chosen to match the services the agents' ancillary-services pot pays for. The agents' market does not hold reserve, so the realised cost carries none of this requirement and the operation gap is understated by its cost (small: the agents' fleet has ample battery and OCGT headroom). Scenario key `reserve` overrides any setting; `{"scale": 0}` switches it off (the "reserve off" sensitivity).
* **Ladder**: agent trait groups are idealised one at a time (`scenarios.IDEAL_TRAITS`): expectations (all agents adaptive, i.e. know the carbon path), capital (WACC = 3.5 %, no risk aversion, no budget limit), strategic (no mark-up). With all three idealised, what remains is the market and plan design (CfD pots and caps, CM target, VRE only through CfDs), the agents' decision procedure (t+3 price forecasts, lumpy projects, at most 6 merchant decisions a year) and path dependence. The ladder is cumulative in one order; other orders give other attributions (interactions), so the steps are indicative.
* Limitations: one weather year; the planner sees the 2026–28 gas spike coming, the agents see it only through forecasts made 3 years ahead; foreign prices do not respond to GB exports, so large export volumes are valued at the exogenous continental price; no network in either run.

## Known inconsistencies (found while writing the parameter register, 28 Nov 2026)
Left as they are in this release because changing them changes results; to be settled together.

* **Interconnector de-rating.** The Capacity Market auction, its T-1 target and the long-horizon
  forecast count interconnectors at 0.45 of capacity (hard-coded); the scarcity mark-up uses
  `config.INTERCONNECTOR_DERATING` = 0.6 (NESO's median for 2030-31).
* **Hinkley Point C strike.** The model uses £92.50/MWh (2012 prices); the register in
  `data/pypsagb_snapshot/` has £89.50 since Sizewell C's investment decision.
* **Value of lost load in the network stage** is hard-coded at £6,000/MWh and does not follow
  `Policy.voll`. `Policy.cm_net_cone` is defined and never used.
* **`AR8_modelled` rows** in the snapshot's CfD strike table are PyPSA-GB model output, not auction
  results; the model does not read them (it filters on awarded capacity).
* **Nuclear new build.** The online years and unit sizes of Hinkley Point C and Sizewell C in
  `model.NUCLEAR_UNITS` have no source in the code; `data/history/sources/closures_openings.csv` has
  EDF's statements on Hinkley Point C.

## Assumptions challenged by the foundations review, and what was done (November 2026)
The [foundations review](review_2026-11_foundations.md) found assumptions that were not written down
here, or whose weight was understated. Finding numbers refer to it.

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| Onshore and offshore wind share one hourly shape (F1) | **Changed.** Separate measured profiles for 2021–2025; new build on its own capacity factor. The planner's wind mix is still bounded by the build-rate limits, which are assumptions. |
| The yardstick: discount rate, carbon value, value of lost load (F2) | **Open.** The social discount rate in the code is 3.5 % (the Green Book rate has been 3.0 % since October 2026); carbon is at the market price path. `scripts/run_planner_sensitivity.py` gives the gap under the alternatives; its results predate the changes on this page. |
| The planner's sample of hours understates scarcity (F3) | **Changed.** Stratified sample with extreme days; verified on the full horizon. No plant outages and one weather year still. |
| Firms' forecasts are the model's own run on the true future (F8) | **Open.** Firms know future fuel, carbon and demand paths. |
| Order of moves (F9) | **Changed.** The order of firms is drawn again before every merchant decision. |
| Costs and hurdle rates (F10) | **Changed.** DESNZ 2025 costs and CEPA hurdle rates. The first offshore strike is £109/MWh against £91 in Round 7 (before: £53–66). Not fitted to the auctions. |
| Capacity Market entrants (F11) | **Partly.** NESO de-rating factors; target from the reliability standard; the first T-4 clears at £38/kW against £27. Still no small engines, demand response or refurbishment. |
| Every award is built, in whole blocks (F12) | **Open.** No non-delivery. |
| Five invented firms (F14) | **Changed.** Twelve firms with the plants their companies own; traits by type remain assumptions. |
| Uncertainty (F21) | **Open.** The parameter register exists; no sensitivity analysis has been run on it. |
| Data licence of the 2024 profile (F28) | **Changed.** Profiles are built from NESO and Elexon open data. The ESPENI-derived file is still in `data/pypsagb_snapshot/` for comparison and should be removed; see the [data register](data.md). |
