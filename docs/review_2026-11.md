# Code review, 27 November 2026

Four independent read-only reviews of the code on `roadmap/07-convergence-test` (operation and
settlement; investment, auctions and institutions; the optimum and the gap; data, calibration and
tests), then checked against the code. **Verified** means the finding was traced and reproduced (toy
inputs or reading the full code path) during the review; **two reviewers** means two reviewers found it
independently. README roadmap item 0 fixes these, highest severity first, before results are re-run.
Every result produced before the fixes carries the "biases results" findings.

## A. Bugs that bias results

| # | where | finding | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | `model.py` `cm_auction` (existing plant bids) | Every existing plant bids £0/kW into the Capacity Market: profit (net of FOM) is floored at 0 before FOM is added back, so `max(fom − (margin + fom), 0) = 0`. Existing plant never sets the clearing price; when zero-priced capacity exceeds the demand curve, plant id order decides who wins; since an agreement blocks loss-based retirement (`cm_end`), thermal retirement follows plant id, not economics. The profit used also includes last year's CM receipts. Since the first model commit. | verified, two reviewers |
| A2 | `model.py` `cm_auction`, `settle` | CM agreements are overwritten and paid early: T-4 offers include plant already holding an agreement (only T-1 skips them), so a new build's 15-year agreement becomes a rolling one-year agreement at each year's clearing price; settlement pays `year <= cm_end` with no start, so existing plant is paid the T-4 price from the year after the auction. CM cost jumps (2026 £2.5bn → 2027 £0.1bn) when one near-zero clearing re-prices the fleet. | two reviewers (three) |
| A3 | `market.py` `valley_fill` | The bisection's upper bound adds hours to MW (`r.max() + E/cap + 1`), so on days when the charging window holds the day's highest residual load, smart-EV energy is not delivered: 0 % lost in 2030, 0.5 % in 2035, ~11 % (4.8 TWh) in 2045. The loss falls in the tightest hours, so 2040s prices, scarcity and the planner's load are too low. | verified |
| A4 | `costs.py` `fixed_costs` | The capex annuity is counted only while a unit is active, so a plant retired early on losses stops costing anything; the planner's units never retire early. Stranded assets lower the agents' measured cost: the investment gap is understated (most in energy-only runs, where new thermal plant has no CM agreement to protect it). | verified |
| A5 | `model.py` `_init_fleet`, `settle` | Renewables Obligation support never ends: half of the initial wind and solar of any vintage (incl. post-2017 plant) is tagged RO and paid £55/MWh until it retires, bidding −£55/MWh. RO closed to new capacity in 2017 and ends in 2037. ~11–14 GW still paid in 2040, ~£1.5bn/yr, ~£4–5/MWh on the levy (feeds retail prices, heat-pump uptake and the government's levy guard). | two reviewers |
| A6 | `model.py` legacy CfD strike | Legacy CfD strike prices are a plain mean over all rows of `cfd_strike_prices_by_round.csv` (incl. 0 MW modelled rows, rounds not yet operating, and Investment Contracts weighted like whole rounds): offshore £157/MWh against ~£113 capacity-weighted over contracts operating by 2025. The 2012→2024 deflator (1.45) is also too high (~1.39). Inflates 2025 CfD cost, the levy and CfD units' BM bids (so the 2025 constraint cost compared with outturn). | reported, to verify |
| A7 | `model.py` `expected_margin_per_mw` | Thermal SRMC in expected margins is taken at year + lead time with the agent's carbon belief, while the price series is for t+3 (or t+8, t+13) on the true paths: e.g. 2025 OCGT margin understated ~£45/MWh (2027 vs 2028 gas); myopic agents' CCGT margin overstated ~£5/MWh. The "expectations" rung of the ladder therefore moves costs, not price beliefs. | reported, to verify |
| A8 | `model.py` `forecast_horizons` (new, item 4) | Long-horizon forecasts are cached for the whole year (stale after the first merchant build, herding with 40 decisions in the convergence benchmark) and use the fleet active at t+3, not at the horizon (keeps retired plant, misses committed later plant such as Sizewell C). | two reviewers |
| A9 | `decisions.py` battery services at horizons (new, item 4) | Battery service revenue at t+8 / t+13 is shared over only the batteries already built and still alive, so it falls to the 5 GW floor (~£60k/MW/yr) while the price fleet grows: battery over-build under the `expectations` logic. | reported, to verify |
| A10 | `model.py` `expected_margin_per_mw` | A flat 0.85 "capture-price haircut" on wind and solar double counts cannibalisation already in the forecast and applies to idealised agents: ~18 % on the required capture price, a bias against merchant renewables in the convergence benchmark. | reported, to verify |
| A11 | `model.py` `invest`, `decisions.py` | Budgets: candidates are checked against the full annual budget, not what is left (firms overspend), and a firm whose best option is unaffordable builds nothing rather than its next best; CfD and CM awards do not count. | reported, to verify |

## B. Inconsistencies

| # | where | finding | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | `model.py` retirement | Gas CCS and hydrogen turbines never retire on losses (only CCGT, OCGT, biomass, battery are tested). | reported |
| B2 | `institutions.py` `SystemOperator`, `model.py` | Merchant and PPA wind and solar (item 4) are neither netted off the CM target nor offered: over-procurement. PPA revenue is never settled (PPA plant earn spot). | reported |

| B6 | `model.py` `forecast_horizons` | Horizon forecasts add peakers to keep the CM margin even when the CM is off, so in the energy-only benchmark the long-horizon prices carry no scarcity rents. | reported |

## C. Calibration honesty and tests

* Several 2025 calibration rows match outturn because parameters were tuned to them (nuclear and
  biomass availability, interconnector price level and spread, the battery pot), but
  `results/calibration_2025.md` presents them like validation. Mark each row calibrated or validated,
  and add an out-of-sample 2024 comparison (the model uses 2024 weather; the outturn table already has
  2024).
* Prices mix real 2024 £ with nominal 2025–26 figures (MID outturn, 2026–27 gas quotes).
* Results carry no provenance (git hash, arguments); stale figures stay in `docs/figures` when a plot is
  skipped; `--end 2030` runs overwrite full-horizon files under the same names.
* Tests that cannot fail or give false confidence: `test_network` constraint cost lower bound set just
  under the current value; `test_calibration` comparison test asserts no metric against outturn;
  `test_convergence` price test (always true); `test_lifetime_investment` merchant-candidate test
  mirrors the code.
* Tests to add first: CM agreement timing; legacy CfD strike; snapshot data integrity (shares sum to 1,
  CFs within DUKES ranges); 2025 calibration with tolerances on independent metrics only plus 2024
  out-of-sample; result provenance.

## D. Planner and gap

| # | where | finding | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | `planner.py` `plan` snapshot weightings | Every snapshot-weighting column, including `stores`, is set to the sampling weight (~6.3–7.0). PyPSA uses the `stores` weighting as the hours elapsed in the storage state-of-charge equation, so each sampled hour moves ~6.5 hours of energy: new batteries deliver at most ~30 % of their rating and pumped hydro acts like ~1.3 h storage. This is why the planner builds no batteries in any run; "agents build 24 GW of batteries the optimum does not need" is an artefact, and the optimum is overstated (gap understated). Fix: `stores` weighting 1, objective and generators at the sampling weight. | verified (code and PyPSA 1.4 source); toy LP by the reviewer |
| D2 | `planner.py` `sample_hours` | `range(3, 365, 7)` samples one weekday only (Thursdays in 2024): base demand +3.5 %, wind +3.6 %, solar −3.2 % against the year; peak days carry a full sampling weight. The planner sizes its fleet for a heavier, windier, less sunny year than it is costed on. Fix: a stride coprime with 7 (or whole weeks / clustering), small weights for peak days, a check that sample means are within ~1 % of the year. | verified (code) |
| D3 | `costs.py` `fixed_costs` | = A4: stranded capex of early-retired agent plant drops out of the agents' cost. | verified |
| D4 | `model.py` retirement, `planner.py` existing fleet | Agents can close loss-making existing plant and save its FOM; the planner must keep the whole initial fleet to natural retirement: optimum overstated, most in no_capacity_market. Fix: existing thermal extendable up to its size with capital cost = FOM. | reported |
| D5 | `gap.py`, `costs.py` | Costs only at 5-yearly period years: agent units online in 2026–29 cost and deliver nothing until 2030; the 2026–27 gas spike is in neither the PV nor the planner. | verified (code) |
| D6 | `scripts/run_convergence.py` | The capacity table's median ignores seeds where a technology is absent (0, 0, 10 GW reports 10). | reported |
| D7 | `gap.py` `run_many` | One failed run (e.g. a solver status) discards the whole batch; results are written only at the end. | reported |
| D8 | `planner.py` `_groups` | The evaluator rounds efficiency to 2 decimals while the realised cost uses exact values: a small artefact in the operation gap. | reported |
| D9 | `make_plots.py`, `docs/gap.md` | Summary tables take independent medians per column, so they do not add up (346 − 315 ≠ 32); the ladder figure stacks medians under a median-total whisker. | reported |
| D10 | `gap.py` `summarise` | Step re-inferred rather than passed; horizons not a multiple of the step silently drop years. | reported |

## What the findings mean for the published numbers

The gap figures in README and `docs/gap.md` (baseline £32bn, 10 %) are affected in both directions:
D1, D2, D4 overstate the optimum's cost (gap understated); A4 understates the agents' cost (gap
understated); A1–A2 change which plant stays and what the CM costs; A5–A6 inflate the levy. The
battery attribution (agents' 24 GW against none) is an artefact of D1. Treat every gap number as
superseded until the fixes are in and the gap is re-run.
